## Findings

1. **major (feel; consequence of the approved pace, for the human gate and triage) — at hand speed most elements are still compressed and the last element of about a third of the scenes is cut off by the yield cap and pops in.** Measured over every fixture scene with the real drawables and the silent provider's durations (`scratchpad/pace-compare.mjs`; real `gpt-4o-mini-tts` durations for 15–90-word narrations are in the same range): of 142 non-last elements **102 (72 %) are compressed**, 36 by more than 3× (list above); only 40 are written at natural speed and parked. Of 87 last elements **33 overrun the audio and 27 overrun it by more than the 1.5 s yield cap**, so the engine snaps them complete: fx-core-dense/s001 `six8` needs 37.1 s in a 13.6 s scene (about 70 % of the list appears at once at the cap), fx-type-svg/s002 `forty` +22.9 s, fx-type-diagram/s002 `max` +21.4 s, fx-long-stroke/s003 `grid` +16.6 s, fx-pic-labels s001–s003 `pipeline` +5.8 / +13.2 / +15.2 s, fx-type-code/s002 `full` +15.1 s, and in the representative lesson fx-full-tour/s006 `costplot` +2.06 s. Under v0.2.2's rule (1500 px/s, stretch to the window) the same fixtures had 35 non-last overruns and **1** last-element snap. The mechanism is exactly what the proposal and the spec delta say ("sped up only as a last resort; the yield-to-pen rule still holds at scene end") and `engine-pace` asserts it, so this is not a defect against the change; it is the trade-off the change makes visible: Sal writes a few words per sentence, these scenes carry a paragraph. Repro at playbackRate 1: `fx-core-dense` s001 or `fx-type-diagram` s002 and watch the end of the scene. Options for triage (not mine to pick): compress the last element to `windowEnd + yieldCap` when it cannot finish (it would then be sped up like the others instead of popping), give the producer a pacing budget in SKILL.md (today it has none: an 8-word body line needs ≈ 4 s after its `at`, a 14-line code block ≈ 28 s, a 6×6 table ≈ 19 s), or weight the default `at` spacing by natural length. The human should judge the feel on a real lesson before archiving (list below).
2. **minor (validator behaviour change, not documented for the producer) — a `list` with `itemAt` starting below 0.08 and no `at` is now rejected with `BAD_AT`.** `checkTiming([{id:'l', type:'list', items:['a','b','c'], itemAt:[0, 0.1, 0.2]}, {id:'t', at:0.5}])` → `BAD_AT` (pinned by `test/foundation/timing.test.js`); valid under v0.2.2. SKILL.md still says "omit it and elements are spaced evenly across the first 85 % of the audio" (§6 "Ids and timing") and the `BAD_AT` row's fix ("omit `at`, or keep values in range and non-decreasing") does not mention the talk-first window or the remedy (`at: 0` explicitly). No SKILL example trips it; a producer copying older habits will. Either document it or clamp the window start to `min(firstAt, itemAt[0])` when `itemAt` is present.
3. **minor (lint gap) — the SKILL.md ↔ constants lint does not cover sketch shapes or plot caps.** `test/renderer-core/feel.test.js` "caps ↔ SKILL.md" checks the text cap, the highlight style list and the `BAD_ENUM`/`CAP_WORDS` rows for those; the sketch row (fifteen shapes), the plot row (`xLabel`/`yLabel` ≤ 2, `labels` ≤ 3, `hole`) and the `CAP_WORDS` plot entries are unchecked. They match today (verified by reading against `constants.js` and `plot.test.js`); the next shape or cap change can drift silently. `test/cli/skill.test.js` has not changed since be4df56.
4. **minor (cosmetic) — the tick number at the positive end of an axis crowds the arrowhead.** fx-type-plot s001: "10" sits against the y-axis arrowhead next to the `y` name; s004 "5" / `f(x)`, s005 "4" / `price`, and s004's x "4" under the right arrowhead (`scratchpad/probe-out/fx-type-plot-s00{1,4,5}-u1.png`). `chooseTicks` includes the range end and the tests check names against tick boxes, not arrowheads.
